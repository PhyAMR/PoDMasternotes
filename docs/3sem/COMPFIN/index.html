About these notes

Scope

These are lecture notes for Stochastic Finance — Financial Mathematics for Data Science (third-semester course, Master in Physics of Data, University of Padova, taught by Prof. Claudio Fontana). The course is a first mathematical treatment of financial derivatives: how they are priced, how their risks are hedged, and which mathematical machinery — no-arbitrage arguments, stochastic processes, stochastic calculus, risk-neutral probabilities — makes the treatment rigorous. Numerical methods and two practitioner lectures connect the theory to how banks and insurers actually work.

Reading the notes

  • Part I (Chapters 1–3): markets and no-arbitrage. Markets, clearing and margins, interest rates and bonds; derivatives and the model-free consequences of no arbitrage (forward prices, put–call parity, option bounds); the binomial model, where hedging, risk-neutral valuation, martingales and American options appear in their simplest form.
  • Part II (Chapters 4–8): continuous-time models. Brownian motion and Itô calculus; the Black–Scholes–Merton model, its PDE and formula, the Greeks and hedging in practice; Monte Carlo methods and variance reduction; local and stochastic volatility; interest rate products and short-rate models.
  • Part III (Chapters 9–10): practitioner lectures. Risk-free rate curves after the end of Libor and their use in insurance; automated market making for FX options.

Each chapter separates definitions, theorems, examples and remarks in styled boxes, includes the derivations done in class, and ends with exercises (solutions collapsible in the HTML version) and a Sources section. Numerical examples were checked by direct computation; where the course slides contain a misprint, the text says so.

Notation

  • \(S_t\): price of the underlying at time \(t\); \(B_t\): bank account; \(P_t(T)\): price at \(t\) of the zero-coupon bond maturing at \(T\).
  • \(r\) (constant) or \(r_t\) (stochastic): continuously compounded risk-free (short) rate; \(R_t(\tau)\): zero rate at \(t\) for horizon \(\tau\).
  • \(W_t\): Brownian motion under \(\mathbb P\); \(W^Q_t\) under the risk-neutral probability \(Q\); \(\mathcal F_t\): information at time \(t\).
  • \(\theta = (\theta^B, \theta^S)\): trading strategy in bank account and stock; \(V_t(\theta)\): its value.
  • \(\Pi_t(G)\): arbitrage-free price at \(t\) of the payoff \(G\); \(F(t, x)\): pricing function.
  • \(\Phi\), \(\varphi\): standard normal distribution function and density.
  • \(\Delta, \Gamma, \Theta, \mathcal V, \rho\): the Greeks; \(\sigma_{\mathrm{imp}}\): implied volatility.
  • \(L_t(S, T)\): simple forward rate; \(f_t(T)\): instantaneous forward rate.
