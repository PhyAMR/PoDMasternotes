5  Brownian motion and stochastic calculus

Continuous-time derivative pricing requires a continuous-time model of the underlying. The paradigm is Brownian motion, and the calculus that handles integrals against it is Itô’s calculus. This chapter is the mathematical backbone of everything from here on.

5.1 Brownian motion

A standard Brownian motion \(\{W_t\}_{t \geq 0}\) on a probability space \((\Omega, \mathcal F, \mathbb P)\) is a stochastic process such that:

  1. \(W_0 = 0\) a.s.
  2. Increments \(W_t - W_s\) are independent of \(\mathcal F_s\) for \(s < t\).
  3. \(W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal N(0, t - s)\) for \(s \leq t\).
  4. \(t \mapsto W_t\) is continuous a.s.

For standard Brownian motion:

  • Martingale. \(\mathbb E[W_t \mid \mathcal F_s] = W_s\) for \(s \leq t\).
  • Quadratic variation. For any \(t > 0\), \([W]_t = t\): the (bracket) accumulates variance at rate one.
  • Nowhere differentiable. With probability one, \(t \mapsto W_t\) is continuous but not differentiable at any point.
  • Scaling invariance. For any \(c > 0\), \(\{c^{-1/2}W_{ct}\}_{t \geq 0}\) is again a standard BM.
  • Reflection principle. For any \(a > 0\), \(P(\max_{s \leq t}W_s \geq a) = 2 P(W_t \geq a)\).

Remark (Physical origin). Brownian motion was named for the pollen-grain jiggling observed by botanist Robert Brown in 1827. Einstein (1905) derived the diffusion equation from the same phenomenon and set the microscopic mean-square displacement at \(\langle x^2\rangle \sim t\). Kolmogorov (1933) axiomatised the mathematical object; Norbert Wiener (1923) constructed it — hence the name “Wiener process”.

5.2 Filtrations and martingales

A filtration \(\{\mathcal F_t\}_{t \geq 0}\) is an increasing family of \(\sigma\)-algebras: \(\mathcal F_s \subseteq \mathcal F_t\) for \(s \leq t\). Intuitively \(\mathcal F_t\) contains all information observable up to time \(t\). A process \(X\) is adapted to \(\{\mathcal F_t\}\) if \(X_t\) is \(\mathcal F_t\)-measurable for every \(t\).

An integrable adapted process \(X\) is a martingale with respect to \(\{\mathcal F_t\}\) if \(\mathbb E[X_t \mid \mathcal F_s] = X_s\) for all \(s \leq t\). A supermartingale has \(\leq\), a submartingale has \(\geq\).

Martingales formalise the “fair game” property: the best forecast of a martingale, given today’s information, is its current value.

The processes \(W_t\), \(W_t^2 - t\), and \(\exp(\theta W_t - \theta^2 t/2)\) (the exponential martingale) are all martingales in the filtration generated by \(W\). These three are the building blocks of most calculations.

5.3 Itô integral

The Itô integral \(\int_0^t H_s\,dW_s\) generalises the Riemann integral to integrators of unbounded variation, exploiting the fact that Brownian motion has finite quadratic variation.

For a piecewise-constant integrand \(H_s = \sum_{k}h_k \mathbf 1_{(t_k, t_{k+1}]}(s)\) with \(h_k\) \(\mathcal F_{t_k}\)-measurable, the Itô integral is \[\int_0^t H_s\,dW_s = \sum_k h_k (W_{t_{k+1} \wedge t} - W_{t_k \wedge t}).\] The definition extends by isometry to general square-integrable adapted \(H\).

For \(H\) square-integrable and adapted, \[\mathbb E\left[\left(\int_0^t H_s\,dW_s\right)^2\right] = \mathbb E\left[\int_0^t H_s^2\,ds\right].\] The Itô integral is a martingale with zero mean.

5.4 Itô’s lemma

The chain rule for stochastic calculus:

Let \(X_t\) satisfy the SDE \(dX_t = \mu_t\,dt + \sigma_t\,dW_t\), and \(f : [0, T] \times \mathbb R \to \mathbb R\) be \(C^{1,2}\). Then \[df(t, X_t) = \left(\partial_t f + \mu_t\,\partial_x f + \tfrac12 \sigma_t^2\,\partial_x^2 f\right)dt + \sigma_t\,\partial_x f\,dW_t .\]

The correction term \(\tfrac12\sigma_t^2 \partial_x^2 f\) is the signature of stochastic calculus: it arises because \((dW_t)^2 = dt\) at second order.

Applying Itô to \(S_t = S_0 \exp(X_t)\) with \(dX_t = \mu\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t\): with \(f(x) = e^x\) we get \[dS_t = S_t\bigl(\mu + \tfrac12 \sigma^2\bigr)dt + S_t\,\sigma\,dW_t .\] The natural log SDE has drift \(\mu\); the level SDE has drift \(\mu + \sigma^2/2\) — the Itô correction.

5.5 SDEs and their solutions

An Itô SDE with drift \(b : [0, T] \times \mathbb R \to \mathbb R\) and diffusion \(\sigma : [0, T] \times \mathbb R \to \mathbb R\) is \[dX_t = b(t, X_t)\,dt + \sigma(t, X_t)\,dW_t, \qquad X_0 = x_0 .\] A strong solution is an \(\mathcal F_t\)-adapted process \(X_t\) satisfying the integral form of the SDE.

If \(b\) and \(\sigma\) are globally Lipschitz in \(x\) (uniformly in \(t\)) with linear growth, then the SDE has a unique strong solution on \([0, T]\).

\(dX_t = \kappa(\bar x - X_t)\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t\): mean-reverting Gaussian process. Explicit solution: \[X_t = X_0 e^{-\kappa t} + \bar x(1 - e^{-\kappa t}) + \sigma \int_0^t e^{-\kappa(t - s)}\,dW_s.\] Asymptotic distribution \(\mathcal N(\bar x, \sigma^2/(2\kappa))\). The OU process is the workhorse of short-rate modelling (Chapter 8) and stochastic volatility (Chapter 7).

5.6 Girsanov’s theorem

The bridge from the physical measure \(\mathbb P\) to a risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb Q\) is a measure change engineered by Girsanov:

Let \(\theta_t\) be adapted with \(\mathbb E\bigl[\exp(\tfrac12\int_0^T \theta_s^2\,ds)\bigr] < \infty\) (Novikov’s condition). Define the exponential martingale \[Z_t = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \theta_s\,dW_s - \tfrac12 \int_0^t \theta_s^2\,ds\right),\qquad \mathbb E[Z_T] = 1.\] Then \(d\mathbb Q / d\mathbb P = Z_T\) defines a probability measure \(\mathbb Q\) equivalent to \(\mathbb P\), and \(\tilde W_t = W_t + \int_0^t \theta_s\,ds\) is a standard \(\mathbb Q\)-Brownian motion.

If \(S\) follows \(dS_t = \mu S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_t\) under \(\mathbb P\), then under \(\mathbb Q\) defined by \(\theta = (\mu - r)/\sigma\) the drift becomes \(r\): \[dS_t = r S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,d\tilde W_t,\] i.e. \(S\) is drifted at the risk-free rate — the essence of risk-neutral valuation.

5.7 Fundamental theorem of asset pricing

An arbitrage-free market with numéraire \(B\) admits an equivalent measure \(\mathbb Q\) under which every discounted asset \(S/B\) is a \(\mathbb Q\)-martingale. Completeness of the market corresponds to uniqueness of \(\mathbb Q\).

Remark. This is the deep, continuous-time analogue of the finite-tree FTAP of Chapter 3. Its proof uses Girsanov to convert away the drift and semi-martingale representation theorems to characterise the set of equivalent measures.

5.8 Exercises

Show that \(M_t = W_t^2 - t\) is a martingale by direct computation.

Solution. For \(s < t\): \(\mathbb E[W_t^2 \mid \mathcal F_s] = \mathbb E[(W_s + (W_t - W_s))^2 \mid \mathcal F_s] = W_s^2 + 2W_s\mathbb E[W_t - W_s \mid \mathcal F_s] + \mathbb E[(W_t - W_s)^2] = W_s^2 + 0 + (t - s)\). So \(\mathbb E[M_t \mid \mathcal F_s] = W_s^2 + (t - s) - t = W_s^2 - s = M_s\). ✓

Apply Itô’s lemma to compute \(d(t W_t)\). Deduce that \(\int_0^t s\,dW_s = t W_t - \int_0^t W_s\,ds\).

Solution. \(f(t, x) = t x\): \(\partial_t f = x\), \(\partial_x f = t\), \(\partial_x^2 f = 0\). So \(d(t W_t) = W_t\,dt + t\,dW_t\). Rearranging: \(\int_0^t s\,dW_s = t W_t - \int_0^t W_s\,ds\). This is integration by parts under the Itô rule.

Compute the mean and variance of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck solution starting from \(X_0 = x_0\). Verify convergence to the stationary distribution.

Solution. \(\mathbb E[X_t] = x_0 e^{-\kappa t} + \bar x(1 - e^{-\kappa t})\); \(\text{Var}(X_t) = \sigma^2 (1 - e^{-2\kappa t})/(2\kappa)\). As \(t \to \infty\): \(\mathbb E \to \bar x\), \(\text{Var} \to \sigma^2/(2\kappa)\). Gaussian stationary distribution \(\mathcal N(\bar x, \sigma^2/(2\kappa))\).

5.9 Callouts

Itô’s lemma is not a chain rule of ordinary calculus with extra terms. The extra \(\tfrac12 \sigma^2 \partial_x^2 f\,dt\) arises because \(dW\) has magnitude \(\sqrt{dt}\), so second-order terms contribute at first order.

When solving an SDE by hand, first try to guess a function \(Y_t = f(t, X_t)\) that satisfies a simpler SDE (constant coefficients, or deterministic). This turns the problem into a straightforward integration.

Stratonovich integrals (\(X \circ dW\)) and Itô integrals (\(X\,dW\)) give different values for stochastic integrals. Finance uses Itô convention; classical physics often uses Stratonovich. Be careful when reading across disciplines.

The convergence of a Monte-Carlo Euler scheme for an SDE is weak: sample paths do not converge to the true path, only their laws do. If you need pathwise convergence, use a Milstein or higher-order scheme.

## Itô integral

::: {.definition name="Itô integral for simple integrands"}
For a piecewise-constant integrand $H_s = \sum_{k}h_k \mathbf 1_{(t_k, t_{k+1}]}(s)$ with $h_k$ $\mathcal F_{t_k}$-measurable, the Itô integral is
$$\int_0^t H_s\,dW_s = \sum_k h_k (W_{t_{k+1} \wedge t} - W_{t_k \wedge t}).$$
The definition extends by isometry to general square-integrable adapted $H$.
:::

::: {.proposition name="Itô isometry"}
For $H$ square-integrable and adapted,
$$\mathbb E\left[\left(\int_0^t H_s\,dW_s\right)^2\right] = \mathbb E\left[\int_0^t H_s^2\,ds\right].$$
The Itô integral is a martingale with zero mean.
:::

## Itô's lemma

The chain rule for stochastic calculus:

::: {.theorem name="Itô's lemma"}
Let $X_t$ satisfy the SDE $dX_t = \mu_t\,dt + \sigma_t\,dW_t$, and $f : [0, T] \times \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ be $C^{1,2}$. Then
$$df(t, X_t) = \left(\partial_t f + \mu_t\,\partial_x f + \tfrac12 \sigma_t^2\,\partial_x^2 f\right)dt + \sigma_t\,\partial_x f\,dW_t .$$
:::

The correction term $\tfrac12\sigma_t^2 \partial_x^2 f$ is *the* signature of stochastic calculus: it arises because $(dW_t)^2 = dt$ at second order.

::: {.example name="Geometric Brownian motion"}
Applying Itô to $S_t = S_0 \exp(X_t)$ with $dX_t = \mu\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t$: with $f(x) = e^x$ we get
$$dS_t = S_t\bigl(\mu + \tfrac12 \sigma^2\bigr)dt + S_t\,\sigma\,dW_t .$$
The natural log SDE has drift $\mu$; the level SDE has drift $\mu + \sigma^2/2$ — the Itô correction.
:::

## SDEs and their solutions

::: {.definition name="Stochastic differential equation"}
An **Itô SDE** with drift $b : [0, T] \times \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ and diffusion $\sigma : [0, T] \times \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ is
$$dX_t = b(t, X_t)\,dt + \sigma(t, X_t)\,dW_t, \qquad X_0 = x_0 .$$
A **strong solution** is an $\mathcal F_t$-adapted process $X_t$ satisfying the integral form of the SDE.
:::

::: {.theorem name="Existence and uniqueness"}
If $b$ and $\sigma$ are globally Lipschitz in $x$ (uniformly in $t$) with linear growth, then the SDE has a unique strong solution on $[0, T]$.
:::

::: {.example name="Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process"}
$dX_t = \kappa(\bar x - X_t)\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t$: mean-reverting Gaussian process. Explicit solution:
$$X_t = X_0 e^{-\kappa t} + \bar x(1 - e^{-\kappa t}) + \sigma \int_0^t e^{-\kappa(t - s)}\,dW_s.$$
Asymptotic distribution $\mathcal N(\bar x, \sigma^2/(2\kappa))$. The OU process is the workhorse of short-rate modelling (Chapter 8) and stochastic volatility (Chapter 7).
:::

## Girsanov's theorem

The bridge from the physical measure $\mathbb P$ to a risk-neutral measure $\mathbb Q$ is a *measure change* engineered by Girsanov:

::: {.theorem name="Girsanov"}
Let $\theta_t$ be adapted with $\mathbb E\bigl[\exp(\tfrac12\int_0^T \theta_s^2\,ds)\bigr] < \infty$ (Novikov's condition). Define the exponential martingale
$$Z_t = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \theta_s\,dW_s - \tfrac12 \int_0^t \theta_s^2\,ds\right),\qquad \mathbb E[Z_T] = 1.$$
Then $d\mathbb Q / d\mathbb P = Z_T$ defines a probability measure $\mathbb Q$ equivalent to $\mathbb P$, and $\tilde W_t = W_t + \int_0^t \theta_s\,ds$ is a standard $\mathbb Q$-Brownian motion.
:::

::: {.corollary name="Change of drift for GBM"}
If $S$ follows $dS_t = \mu S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_t$ under $\mathbb P$, then under $\mathbb Q$ defined by $\theta = (\mu - r)/\sigma$ the drift becomes $r$:
$$dS_t = r S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,d\tilde W_t,$$
i.e.\ $S$ is drifted at the *risk-free rate* — the essence of risk-neutral valuation.
:::

## Fundamental theorem of asset pricing

::: {.theorem name="FTAP (continuous-time)"}
An arbitrage-free market with numéraire $B$ admits an equivalent measure $\mathbb Q$ under which every discounted asset $S/B$ is a $\mathbb Q$-martingale. Completeness of the market corresponds to uniqueness of $\mathbb Q$.
:::

::: {.remark}
This is the deep, continuous-time analogue of the finite-tree FTAP of Chapter 3. Its proof uses Girsanov to convert away the drift and semi-martingale representation theorems to characterise the set of equivalent measures.
:::
