About these notes

Scope

These are lecture notes for Physics of Complex Systems (third-semester course, Master in Physics of Data, University of Padova). A complex system is one whose large-scale behaviour cannot be read off the equations of its individual constituents: it emerges from the collective dynamics of very many degrees of freedom, most of which we can neither follow nor control. The natural language for such systems is that of stochastic processes and nonequilibrium statistical physics — we give up on individual trajectories and describe instead how probabilities evolve in time.

The course moves through the following topics:

  1. Brownian motion, stochastic differential equations and stochastic processes (Langevin and Fokker–Planck descriptions).
  2. Markov chains and the detailed-balance condition; equilibrium and nonequilibrium steady states (NESS).
  3. Linear response theory and transport; dynamic susceptibility, the fluctuation–dissipation theorem and the Kramers–Kronig relations.
  4. Onsager theory of coupled transport (reciprocity, Seebeck and Peltier effects); fluctuation theorems, work identities, generalised detailed balance and entropy production.
  5. Nonequilibrium phase transitions: absorbing phases and directed percolation; driven systems and the asymmetric simple exclusion process.
  6. Information theory and the data-transmission problem: Shannon’s coding theorem, the random energy model and random code ensembles.

Reading the notes

The notes are written lecture by lecture and grow with the course.

  • Part I (Chapters 1–3): from the microscopic picture of a particle kicked by molecules to the probabilistic description of diffusion. Chapter 1 builds the random walk, Einstein’s theory of Brownian motion and the Langevin equation, and sets up the master equation. Chapter 2 derives the Fokker–Planck equation, reads it as a continuity equation for probability, distinguishes stationarity from equilibrium, and closes with the Chapman–Kolmogorov equation and its forward/backward Kolmogorov forms. Chapter 3 uses the backward equation to compute mean first exit times exactly, derives the Kramers–Arrhenius formula for the escape from a metastable well, and applies the same tools to the translocation of a polymer through a pore.

Every chapter uses the styled callout environments to separate definitions, results, worked examples and remarks from the surrounding prose, and ends with exercises (solutions collapsible in the HTML version) and a Sources section. Diagrams are used wherever they make the physics visible: separation of time scales, balance of currents, spreading of probability, gain and loss of probability in the master equation.

Notation and conventions

  • Temperature in energy units: \(k_B = 1\) throughout, so \(T\) is measured in joules and \(k_B T \to T\).
  • \(\langle \cdot \rangle\): average over realisations of the noise (equivalently, over an ensemble of replicas).
  • \(\tilde\gamma\): friction coefficient (force per unit velocity), \(\tilde\gamma = 6\pi\eta R\) for a sphere in the Stokes regime; \(\gamma = \tilde\gamma / m = 1/t_d\) the corresponding relaxation rate.
  • \(\tilde\eta(t)\): stochastic force; \(\eta(t) = \tilde\eta(t)/m\) the stochastic force per unit mass; noise strengths \(\tilde\Gamma\) and \(\Gamma = \tilde\Gamma/m^2\).
  • \(D\): diffusion coefficient; \(d\): spatial dimension.
  • \(p(\mathbf x, t)\): probability density of the state \(\mathbf x\) at time \(t\).
  • \(R(\mathbf x, \mathbf x')\): transition rate from \(\mathbf x'\) to \(\mathbf x\) (probability per unit time and per unit volume). Warning: Livi–Politi write \(W(\mathbf x, \mathbf x')\) for the same object in §1.4.2; we use \(R\) from the start, consistently with the continuous-time master equation of §1.6, where \(R_{ik}\) is the rate from state \(k\) to state \(i\).
  • \(W(x_0, t_0 \mid x, t)\): transition probability density to be at \(x\) at time \(t\) given \(x_0\) at time \(t_0\) (time flows from left to right).
  • \(\mathbf J(\mathbf x, t)\): probability current.
  • \(\mathbb P_{x_0}(t)\): survival probability in an interval \(I\) starting from \(x_0\); \(\mathcal T_I(x_0)\): first exit time from \(I\) (a random variable, calligraphic to distinguish it from the temperature \(T\)), with density \(\Pi(t) = -\partial_t\mathbb P_{x_0}(t)\) and mean \(\langle\mathcal T(x_0)\rangle\).
  • \(\Phi(x) = \exp\big[\frac1D\int^x dy\;a(y)\big]\): integrating factor of the mean-exit-time equation (the Boltzmann factor \(e^{-U/T}\) for a conservative force).
