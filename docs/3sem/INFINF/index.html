Information Theory and Inference

From Shannon entropy to Hamiltonian Monte Carlo — Lecture Notes

Author

Alvaro Mendez

Published

October 8, 2026

0.1 Scope

These are lecture notes for Information Theory and Inference (third-semester elective, Master in Physics of Data, University of Padova, instructor Prof. Michele Allegra). The course starts from Shannon’s operational definition of information and ends with modern Markov-Chain Monte Carlo methods for high-dimensional Bayesian inference.

The two halves of the course share a single organising object: the probability distribution. In Part I we ask how much information such a distribution contains, and how many bits are needed to communicate outcomes over a noisy channel. In Part II we ask, given a small amount of data, how to sample from a posterior distribution we can evaluate only up to an unknown normalisation constant.

0.2 Reading the notes

Chapters 1–5 build up Shannon’s toolbox: surprisal, entropy, KL divergence, mutual information, the two coding theorems (source and noisy-channel), and their continuous counterparts. Chapter 6 extends information to time series and introduces the Markov-chain theory that powers Part II. Chapters 7–8 develop Monte Carlo sampling: Metropolis, convergence and autocorrelation, Gibbs sampling with latent variables. Chapter 9 applies them to a real high-dimensional problem (the origin of grey-seal founders). Chapters 10–12 cover the alternatives and extensions: variational Bayes (and the variational autoencoder), Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, and approximate Bayesian computation for models whose likelihood can only be simulated.

Every chapter uses the styled callout environments (definition, theorem, proposition, lemma, corollary, example, remark, note, exercise, solution) so that key results can be scanned without reading the surrounding prose.

0.3 Notation

  • \(X, Y, Z\): random variables; \(x, y, z\) realisations; \(\mathcal X\) their alphabet.
  • \(p_X(x) = P(X = x)\), \(p(x, y)\): probability mass or density; \(p(x \mid y)\) conditional.
  • \(H(X)\), \(H(X, Y)\), \(H(X \mid Y)\): entropy, joint entropy, conditional entropy.
  • \(I(X; Y)\): mutual information; \(D(p \| q)\): Kullback–Leibler divergence.
  • \(C\): channel capacity; \(R\): code rate.
  • \(\pi(\mathbf x)\): target distribution in MCMC; \(T(\mathbf x \to \mathbf x')\): transition kernel; \(q(\mathbf x' \mid \mathbf x)\): proposal.

0.4 Sources

These notes are a re-organised version of Notes on Information Theory and Inference by Prof. Michele Allegra (University of Padova, 2025), with additional material from the two standard textbooks:

  • Thomas M. Cover & Joy A. Thomas, Elements of Information Theory, 2nd ed., Wiley, 2006.
  • David J. C. MacKay, Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms, Cambridge, 2003.

Chapters 7–8 also draw on Steve Brooks, Andrew Gelman, Galin Jones & Xiao-Li Meng (eds.), Handbook of Markov Chain Monte Carlo, CRC Press, 2011.

Every chapter closes with its own Sources section naming the specific sections used.
