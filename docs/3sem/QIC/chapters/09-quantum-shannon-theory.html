9  Classical and quantum Shannon theory

How much information does a quantum system carry? A qubit has a continuum of states, yet intuition suggests it cannot store more than one bit retrievably. This chapter recalls the classical entropies of Shannon — compression rate, mutual information, channel capacity — introduces their quantum counterpart, the von Neumann entropy, highlights where quantum and classical behaviour part ways (negative conditional entropy), and proves the operational answer: the Holevo bound on accessible information.

9.1 Classical information theory in brief

For a random variable \(X\) with distribution \(p_x\), \[H(X) = -\sum_x p_x\log_2 p_x .\]

\(H(X)\) is the average surprise of an outcome and, operationally, the compression rate: by Shannon’s noiseless coding theorem, \(n\) i.i.d. copies of \(X\) can be compressed into about \(nH(X)\) bits and no fewer, for \(n \to \infty\). It ranges from \(0\) (deterministic) to \(\log_2|X|\) (uniform). For a binary variable with \(p(1) = Q\) it is the binary entropy \(h_2(Q) = -Q\log_2 Q - (1-Q)\log_2(1-Q)\).

For two variables:

  • joint entropy \(H(X, Y) = -\sum_{x,y}p_{xy}\log_2 p_{xy}\);
  • conditional entropy \(H(X|Y) = H(X,Y) - H(Y)\): the additional bits needed to specify \(X\) when \(Y\) is known — the residual ignorance about \(X\);
  • mutual information \(I(X:Y) = H(X) - H(X|Y) = H(Y) - H(Y|X) = H(X) + H(Y) - H(X,Y)\): how much learning \(Y\) reduces the bits needed to specify \(X\).

Figure: entropies of two variables. The union is \(H(X,Y)\); classically every region is non-negative.

A channel defined by conditional probabilities \(p(y|x)\) has capacity \[C = \max_{p_x} I(X:Y).\] Every rate \(R < C\) can be transmitted with vanishing error probability, and no rate \(R > C\) can.

For the binary symmetric channel with flip probability \(Q\), \(C = 1 - h_2(Q)\), achieved by uniform inputs. This formula reappears in QKD as the information Alice and Bob share through a channel with error rate \(Q\).

9.2 Von Neumann entropy

\[S(\hat\rho) = -\mathrm{Tr}[\hat\rho\log_2\hat\rho] = -\sum_j\lambda_j\log_2\lambda_j,\] where \(\lambda_j\) are the eigenvalues of \(\hat\rho\): the Shannon entropy of its spectrum.

  1. \(S(\hat\rho) = 0\) iff \(\hat\rho\) is pure.
  2. Unitary invariance: \(S(\hat U\hat\rho\hat U^\dagger) = S(\hat\rho)\).
  3. \(0 \leq S(\hat\rho) \leq \log_2 d\), with the maximum for \(\hat{\mathbb 1}/d\).
  4. Concavity: \(S(\sum_k p_k\hat\rho_k) \geq \sum_k p_k S(\hat\rho_k)\) — mixing increases ignorance.
  5. Subadditivity: \(S(AB) \leq S(A) + S(B)\), with equality iff \(\hat\rho_{AB} = \hat\rho_A\otimes\hat\rho_B\) (as for Shannon entropy).
  6. Araki–Lieb (triangle) inequality: \(S(AB) \geq |S(A) - S(B)|\).
  7. For a pure bipartite state, \(S(A) = S(B)\) (both reduced states have the Schmidt coefficients as spectrum).

For a qubit with Bloch vector \(\vec r\), \(S = h_2\bigl(\tfrac{1 + |\vec r|}{2}\bigr)\).

Point 6 departs from the classical relation \(H(X,Y) \geq \max(H(X), H(Y))\). For a Bell state, \(S(AB) = 0\) while \(S(A) = S(B) = 1\): the whole is less uncertain than its parts. Consequently the quantum conditional entropy \[S(A|B) = S(AB) - S(B)\] can be negative: \(S(A|B) = -1\) for a Bell state. Negative conditional entropy is a signature of entanglement (it is impossible for separable states) and has an operational meaning in quantum state merging. For pure states, \(S(A) = S(B)\) is the standard measure of entanglement, the entanglement entropy.

\(I(A:B) = S(A) + S(B) - S(AB) \geq 0\). For a Bell state \(I(A:B) = 2\): twice the classical maximum for two bits.

9.3 Accessible information and the Holevo bound

Alice encodes a classical letter \(x\) (probability \(p_x\)) into a state \(\hat\rho_x\) and sends it to Bob, who performs a POVM \(\{\hat F_y\}\). The outcome statistics are \(p(y|x) = \mathrm{Tr}[\hat F_y\hat\rho_x]\) (after the channel, if any). The accessible information is the best mutual information Bob can achieve: \[I_{acc} = \max_{\{\hat F_y\}} I(X:Y) = \max_{\{\hat F_y\}}\bigl[H(Y) - H(Y|X)\bigr].\]

For the ensemble \(\{p_x, \hat\rho_x\}\) with average state \(\hat\rho = \sum_x p_x\hat\rho_x\), \[I_{acc} \leq \chi \equiv S(\hat\rho) - \sum_x p_x S(\hat\rho_x).\]

Since \(\chi \leq S(\hat\rho) \leq \log_2 d\), by sending \(n\) qubits Alice can convey at most \(n\) bits of classical information — despite the continuum of states a qubit can occupy. For pure states \(\chi = S(\hat\rho)\), and the bound coincides with Schumacher’s quantum compression rate. The bound is generally not attained by measuring letter by letter, but it is achievable asymptotically with block coding (Holevo–Schumacher–Westmoreland).

Alice sends \(\ket{\theta_{0,1}} = \cos\tfrac\theta2\ket 0 \pm \sin\tfrac\theta2\ket 1\) with equal probabilities. The average state is diagonal, \(\hat\rho = \cos^2\tfrac\theta2\ket 0\bra 0 + \sin^2\tfrac\theta2\ket 1\bra 1\), so \[\chi = S(\hat\rho) = h_2\Bigl(\cos^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) \leq 1 .\] Bob measures with a projective measurement \(\hat\Pi_\pm = \tfrac12(\hat{\mathbb 1} \pm \vec n\cdot\hat{\vec\sigma})\); by symmetry the optimum is \(\vec n = \hat x\), for which \(p(\pm|\theta_0) = \tfrac12(1 \pm \sin\theta)\): a binary symmetric channel with error \(\tfrac12(1 - \sin\theta)\), so \[I(X:Y) = 1 - h_2\Bigl(\frac{1 - \sin\theta}{2}\Bigr) \leq h_2\Bigl(\frac{1 + \cos\theta}{2}\Bigr) = \chi,\] with equality only for orthogonal states (\(\theta = \pi/2\)), where both equal \(1\).

Alice sends \(\ket{\phi_0} = \ket 0\), \(\ket{\phi_{1,2}} = \tfrac12\ket 0 \pm \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\ket 1\) with probability \(\tfrac13\) each — three states at \(120°\) on a great circle of the Bloch sphere. Since \(\hat\rho = \hat{\mathbb 1}/2\), the Holevo quantity is \(\chi = 1\). The optimal measurement is the “anti-trine” POVM \[\hat F_y = \tfrac23\ket{\phi_y^\perp}\bra{\phi_y^\perp},\] with \(\ket{\phi_0^\perp} = \ket 1\), \(\ket{\phi_{1,2}^\perp} = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\ket 0 \mp \tfrac12\ket 1\). Outcome \(y\) excludes state \(y\): \(p(y|x) = 0\) for \(y = x\) and \(\tfrac12\) otherwise. Then \(H(X) = H(Y) = \log_2 3\) and \(H(X,Y) = \log_2 3 + 1\), giving \[I_{acc} = \log_2 3 - 1 \approx 0.585 < \chi = 1 .\] A three-outcome POVM on a qubit beats any two-outcome projective measurement here — a concrete advantage of generalized measurements.

9.4 Exercises

Compute \(S(A)\), \(S(B)\), \(S(AB)\), \(S(A|B)\) and \(I(A:B)\) for the Werner state \(\hat\rho_W = p\ket{\Psi^-}\bra{\Psi^-} + (1-p)\hat{\mathbb 1}/4\) and find when \(S(A|B) < 0\).

Solution. Marginals are \(\hat{\mathbb 1}/2\): \(S(A) = S(B) = 1\). Eigenvalues of \(\hat\rho_W\): \(\tfrac{1 + 3p}{4}\) once and \(\tfrac{1-p}{4}\) three times, so \(S(AB) = -\tfrac{1+3p}{4}\log_2\tfrac{1+3p}{4} - \tfrac{3(1-p)}{4}\log_2\tfrac{1-p}{4}\). Then \(S(A|B) = S(AB) - 1\) and \(I(A:B) = 2 - S(AB)\). \(S(A|B) < 0\) iff \(S(AB) < 1\), which happens for \(p \gtrsim 0.75\) — a stricter condition than entanglement (\(p > \tfrac13\)): negative conditional entropy implies entanglement, not vice versa.

Show that for the BB84 states \(\{\ket 0, \ket 1, \ket +, \ket -\}\) sent with equal probability, \(\chi = 1\), and that Bob measuring in the \(z\) basis obtains \(I(X:Y) = \tfrac12\) bit about the four-valued letter.

Solution. \(\hat\rho = \hat{\mathbb 1}/2\) and all states are pure: \(\chi = 1\). Measuring \(z\): for \(x \in \{0, 1\}\) Bob learns \(x\); for \(x \in \{+, -\}\) he gets a uniformly random bit. \(H(X) = 2\), \(H(X|Y) = 1.5\) (given \(y\), \(x\) is \(y\) with probability \(\tfrac12\) or one of \(\pm\) with probability \(\tfrac14\) each), so \(I = 0.5\) bit — far below \(\chi\), and still below the \(1\) bit Bob could get if the basis were announced.

## Von Neumann entropy

::: {.definition name="Von Neumann entropy"}
$$S(\hat\rho) = -\mathrm{Tr}[\hat\rho\log_2\hat\rho] = -\sum_j\lambda_j\log_2\lambda_j,$$
where $\lambda_j$ are the eigenvalues of $\hat\rho$: the Shannon entropy of its spectrum.
:::

::: {.proposition name="Properties"}
1. $S(\hat\rho) = 0$ iff $\hat\rho$ is pure.
2. **Unitary invariance:** $S(\hat U\hat\rho\hat U^\dagger) = S(\hat\rho)$.
3. $0 \leq S(\hat\rho) \leq \log_2 d$, with the maximum for $\hat{\mathbb 1}/d$.
4. **Concavity:** $S(\sum_k p_k\hat\rho_k) \geq \sum_k p_k S(\hat\rho_k)$ — mixing increases ignorance.
5. **Subadditivity:** $S(AB) \leq S(A) + S(B)$, with equality iff $\hat\rho_{AB} = \hat\rho_A\otimes\hat\rho_B$ (as for Shannon entropy).
6. **Araki–Lieb (triangle) inequality:** $S(AB) \geq |S(A) - S(B)|$.
7. For a **pure bipartite state**, $S(A) = S(B)$ (both reduced states have the Schmidt coefficients as spectrum).
:::

For a qubit with Bloch vector $\vec r$, $S = h_2\bigl(\tfrac{1 + |\vec r|}{2}\bigr)$.

Point 6 departs from the classical relation $H(X,Y) \geq \max(H(X), H(Y))$. For a Bell state, $S(AB) = 0$ while $S(A) = S(B) = 1$: the whole is less uncertain than its parts. Consequently the **quantum conditional entropy**
$$S(A|B) = S(AB) - S(B)$$
can be **negative**: $S(A|B) = -1$ for a Bell state. Negative conditional entropy is a signature of entanglement (it is impossible for separable states) and has an operational meaning in quantum state merging. For pure states, $S(A) = S(B)$ is the standard measure of entanglement, the **entanglement entropy**.

::: {.definition name="Quantum mutual information"}
$I(A:B) = S(A) + S(B) - S(AB) \geq 0$. For a Bell state $I(A:B) = 2$: twice the classical maximum for two bits.
:::

## Accessible information and the Holevo bound

Alice encodes a classical letter $x$ (probability $p_x$) into a state $\hat\rho_x$ and sends it to Bob, who performs a POVM $\{\hat F_y\}$. The outcome statistics are $p(y|x) = \mathrm{Tr}[\hat F_y\hat\rho_x]$ (after the channel, if any). The **accessible information** is the best mutual information Bob can achieve:
$$I_{acc} = \max_{\{\hat F_y\}} I(X:Y) = \max_{\{\hat F_y\}}\bigl[H(Y) - H(Y|X)\bigr].$$

::: {.theorem name="Holevo bound (1973)"}
For the ensemble $\{p_x, \hat\rho_x\}$ with average state $\hat\rho = \sum_x p_x\hat\rho_x$,
$$I_{acc} \leq \chi \equiv S(\hat\rho) - \sum_x p_x S(\hat\rho_x).$$
:::

Since $\chi \leq S(\hat\rho) \leq \log_2 d$, **by sending $n$ qubits Alice can convey at most $n$ bits of classical information** — despite the continuum of states a qubit can occupy. For pure states $\chi = S(\hat\rho)$, and the bound coincides with Schumacher's quantum compression rate. The bound is generally not attained by measuring letter by letter, but it is achievable asymptotically with block coding (Holevo–Schumacher–Westmoreland).

::: {.example name="Two non-orthogonal pure states"}
Alice sends $\ket{\theta_{0,1}} = \cos\tfrac\theta2\ket 0 \pm \sin\tfrac\theta2\ket 1$ with equal probabilities. The average state is diagonal, $\hat\rho = \cos^2\tfrac\theta2\ket 0\bra 0 + \sin^2\tfrac\theta2\ket 1\bra 1$, so
$$\chi = S(\hat\rho) = h_2\Bigl(\cos^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) \leq 1 .$$
Bob measures with a projective measurement $\hat\Pi_\pm = \tfrac12(\hat{\mathbb 1} \pm \vec n\cdot\hat{\vec\sigma})$; by symmetry the optimum is $\vec n = \hat x$, for which $p(\pm|\theta_0) = \tfrac12(1 \pm \sin\theta)$: a binary symmetric channel with error $\tfrac12(1 - \sin\theta)$, so
$$I(X:Y) = 1 - h_2\Bigl(\frac{1 - \sin\theta}{2}\Bigr) \leq h_2\Bigl(\frac{1 + \cos\theta}{2}\Bigr) = \chi,$$
with equality only for orthogonal states ($\theta = \pi/2$), where both equal $1$.
:::

::: {.example name="The trine"}
Alice sends $\ket{\phi_0} = \ket 0$, $\ket{\phi_{1,2}} = \tfrac12\ket 0 \pm \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\ket 1$ with probability $\tfrac13$ each — three states at $120°$ on a great circle of the Bloch sphere. Since $\hat\rho = \hat{\mathbb 1}/2$, the Holevo quantity is $\chi = 1$. The optimal measurement is the "anti-trine" POVM
$$\hat F_y = \tfrac23\ket{\phi_y^\perp}\bra{\phi_y^\perp},$$
with $\ket{\phi_0^\perp} = \ket 1$, $\ket{\phi_{1,2}^\perp} = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\ket 0 \mp \tfrac12\ket 1$. Outcome $y$ *excludes* state $y$: $p(y|x) = 0$ for $y = x$ and $\tfrac12$ otherwise. Then $H(X) = H(Y) = \log_2 3$ and $H(X,Y) = \log_2 3 + 1$, giving
$$I_{acc} = \log_2 3 - 1 \approx 0.585 < \chi = 1 .$$
A three-outcome POVM on a qubit beats any two-outcome projective measurement here — a concrete advantage of generalized measurements.
:::
