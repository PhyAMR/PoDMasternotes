6  Nonlocality and Bell inequalities

Entangled states produce correlations between distant measurements. Are they merely the correlations of a pair of socks — determined at the source and revealed later — or something no classical mechanism can mimic? Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen argued for the first view; Bell showed that it makes testable predictions that quantum mechanics violates. This chapter starts from a vivid paradox (the “quantum cakes”), then states the EPR argument, local hidden variables, and the CHSH inequality with its quantum violation.

6.1 The quantum cakes

Two ovens on a conveyor belt bake one cake each, for Alice and Bob. On each cake one can test two incompatible properties: open the oven midway and see whether the cake has Risen early or Not (which ruins the cooking), or wait until the end and taste whether it is Good or Bad. Each tester chooses one of the two tests, at random. After many repetitions:

  1. when both check midway, both cakes have risen early \(9\%\) of the time;
  2. whenever Alice’s cake rose early, Bob’s tasted good;
  3. whenever Bob’s cake rose early, Alice’s tasted good.

How often should both cakes taste good when both wait? Classical reasoning: in the \(9\%\) of runs where both cakes rise, the properties “would” also make both good — by (2) and (3) — so \(P(G_A, G_B) \geq 9\%\). Quantum mechanically one can have \(P(G_A, G_B) = 0\).

Encode \(\ket G = \ket 0\), \(\ket B = \ket 1\) and the midway test in the rotated basis \[\ket R = \sqrt{\tfrac25}\ket 0 + \sqrt{\tfrac35}\ket 1, \qquad \ket N = \sqrt{\tfrac35}\ket 0 - \sqrt{\tfrac25}\ket 1,\] and prepare \[\ket\psi = \frac12\ket{11} - \sqrt{\tfrac38}\bigl(\ket{01} + \ket{10}\bigr).\] Then \(\braket{GG|\psi} = 0\), so \(P(G_A, G_B) = 0\); \(\braket{R B|\psi} = \tfrac12\sqrt{\tfrac35} - \sqrt{\tfrac38}\sqrt{\tfrac25} = 0\), so a risen cake on one side implies a good one on the other; and \(\braket{RR|\psi} = \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac35 - 2\sqrt{\tfrac38}\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt6}{5} = -0.3\), so \(P(R_A, R_B) = 0.09\).

The paradox (Hardy, 1993; popularised by Kwiat and Hardy, 2000) disappears only if we give up assigning definite values to properties that were not measured. “Taste” and “rising” are incompatible, and there is no fact of the matter about the taste of a cake that was opened midway.

6.2 EPR and local hidden variables

Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen (1935) considered a maximally entangled pair. If Alice measures \(\hat\sigma_z\) she can predict with certainty Bob’s \(\hat\sigma_z\) result; if she measures \(\hat\sigma_x\), Bob’s \(\hat\sigma_x\) result. Assuming

  • realism: if the value of a quantity can be predicted with certainty without disturbing the system, it corresponds to an element of reality;
  • locality: Alice’s choice cannot disturb Bob’s distant particle,

Bob’s particle must carry definite values of both \(\sigma_z\) and \(\sigma_x\) — which quantum mechanics, forbidding simultaneous eigenstates, does not describe. EPR concluded that quantum mechanics is incomplete.

The natural completion is a local hidden-variable (LHV) model: the source emits a hidden variable \(\lambda\) with distribution \(\rho(\lambda)\); Alice’s outcome \(A(a, \lambda) = \pm1\) depends only on her setting \(a\) and \(\lambda\), Bob’s \(B(b, \lambda) = \pm 1\) only on \(b\) and \(\lambda\). Correlations are \[E(a, b) = \int d\lambda\,\rho(\lambda)\,A(a, \lambda)\,B(b, \lambda).\] Such models reproduce perfect anticorrelation in any single basis. Bell (1964) showed they cannot reproduce all quantum correlations.

6.3 The CHSH inequality

Figure: a Bell test. A source distributes pairs; each party chooses one of two settings at random and records a \(\pm1\) outcome.

For every local hidden-variable model, \[S = E(a,b) + E(a,b') + E(a',b) - E(a',b') \quad\text{satisfies}\quad |S| \leq 2 .\]

Proof. For fixed \(\lambda\), write \(A = A(a,\lambda)\), \(A' = A(a',\lambda)\), \(B = B(b,\lambda)\), \(B' = B(b',\lambda)\), all \(\pm1\). Then \[AB + AB' + A'B - A'B' = A(B + B') + A'(B - B').\] Since \(B, B' = \pm 1\), one of \(B + B'\), \(B - B'\) is \(0\) and the other \(\pm 2\); so the expression is \(\pm 2\). Averaging over \(\rho(\lambda)\) gives \(|S| \leq 2\). \(\square\)

The proof uses only that all four outcomes \(A, A', B, B'\) exist for each run (realism) and that \(A\) does not depend on \(b\) (locality).

For the singlet, \(E(\vec a, \vec b) = \langle(\vec a\cdot\hat{\vec\sigma})\otimes(\vec b\cdot\hat{\vec\sigma})\rangle = -\vec a\cdot\vec b\). With coplanar settings \(\vec a = \hat z\), \(\vec a' = \hat x\), \(\vec b = (\hat z + \hat x)/\sqrt2\), \(\vec b' = (\hat z - \hat x)/\sqrt2\), \[S = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} = -2\sqrt2, \qquad |S| = 2\sqrt2 > 2 .\]

For any quantum state and any \(\pm1\)-valued observables, \(|S| \leq 2\sqrt2\).

Proof sketch. With \(\hat C = \hat A\otimes(\hat B + \hat B') + \hat A'\otimes(\hat B - \hat B')\) and \(\hat A^2 = \hat B^2 = \hat{\mathbb 1}\), one computes \(\hat C^2 = 4\hat{\mathbb 1} - [\hat A, \hat A']\otimes[\hat B, \hat B']\), whose norm is at most \(8\); hence \(|\langle\hat C\rangle| \leq 2\sqrt2\). \(\square\)

Quantum correlations are therefore nonlocal: no local realistic model reproduces them. They are also limited — the no-signalling principle alone would allow \(|S| = 4\) (“PR boxes”).

6.4 Instantaneous collapse without signalling

Alice’s measurement collapses Bob’s conditional state instantaneously, whatever the distance. Does this allow faster-than-light communication? No: as shown in Chapter 5, Bob’s reduced state — and hence every statistic he can observe — is independent of Alice’s choice of measurement. The nonlocal correlations become visible only when Alice and Bob compare their records through a classical (light-speed limited) channel. Nonlocality and relativity coexist peacefully.

Experimental Bell tests began with Freedman–Clauser (1972) and Aspect (1982). Two loopholes long remained open: locality (settings chosen and outcomes recorded within light-cone separation) and detection (losing pairs can fake violations). Both were closed simultaneously in 2015 (Delft, Vienna, NIST). The 2022 Nobel prize in Physics went to Aspect, Clauser and Zeilinger. A CHSH violation is also a resource: it certifies entanglement and randomness without trusting the devices (device-independent QKD and QRNG, Chapter 10).

6.5 Exercises

Show that for the Bell state \(\ket{\Phi^+}\), \(E(\vec a, \vec b) = a_x b_x - a_y b_y + a_z b_z\), and find settings in the \(xz\)-plane achieving \(|S| = 2\sqrt2\).

Solution. \(\langle\hat\sigma_j\otimes\hat\sigma_k\rangle_{\Phi^+}\) is \(1\) for \(xx\) and \(zz\), \(-1\) for \(yy\), \(0\) otherwise. In the \(xz\) plane \(E = \vec a\cdot\vec b = \cos(\theta_a - \theta_b)\). Take \(\theta_a = 0\), \(\theta_{a'} = \pi/2\), \(\theta_b = \pi/4\), \(\theta_{b'} = -\pi/4\): \(S = \cos\tfrac\pi4 + \cos\tfrac\pi4 + \cos\tfrac\pi4 - \cos\tfrac{3\pi}4 = 2\sqrt2\).

Construct an explicit LHV model reproducing the singlet correlations when both parties measure only along \(z\).

Solution. Let \(\lambda = \pm 1\) with probability \(\tfrac12\), \(A(z, \lambda) = \lambda\), \(B(z, \lambda) = -\lambda\). Each outcome is uniformly random and \(E(z,z) = -1\). The model fails as soon as several settings are used, as CHSH proves.

## The CHSH inequality

```{=latex}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[font=\small, line width=0.4pt, >=stealth]
  \node[draw, minimum width=1.1cm, minimum height=0.8cm] (A) at (-3,0) {Alice};
  \node[draw, minimum width=1.1cm, minimum height=0.8cm] (B) at (3,0) {Bob};
  \node[draw, circle, inner sep=2pt] (S) at (0,0) {$S$};
  \draw[->] (S) -- (A);
  \draw[->] (S) -- (B);
  \draw[->] (-3,1.15) node[above] {$a$ or $a'$} -- (A.north);
  \draw[->] (3,1.15) node[above] {$b$ or $b'$} -- (B.north);
  \draw[->] (A.south) -- (-3,-1.15) node[below] {$A = \pm1$};
  \draw[->] (B.south) -- (3,-1.15) node[below] {$B = \pm1$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
```

*Figure: a Bell test. A source distributes pairs; each party chooses one of two settings at random and records a $\pm1$ outcome.*

::: {.theorem name="CHSH inequality (Clauser, Horne, Shimony, Holt, 1969)"}
For every local hidden-variable model,
$$S = E(a,b) + E(a,b') + E(a',b) - E(a',b') \quad\text{satisfies}\quad |S| \leq 2 .$$
:::

*Proof.* For fixed $\lambda$, write $A = A(a,\lambda)$, $A' = A(a',\lambda)$, $B = B(b,\lambda)$, $B' = B(b',\lambda)$, all $\pm1$. Then
$$AB + AB' + A'B - A'B' = A(B + B') + A'(B - B').$$
Since $B, B' = \pm 1$, one of $B + B'$, $B - B'$ is $0$ and the other $\pm 2$; so the expression is $\pm 2$. Averaging over $\rho(\lambda)$ gives $|S| \leq 2$. $\square$

The proof uses only that all four outcomes $A, A', B, B'$ *exist* for each run (realism) and that $A$ does not depend on $b$ (locality).

::: {.proposition name="Quantum violation"}
For the singlet, $E(\vec a, \vec b) = \langle(\vec a\cdot\hat{\vec\sigma})\otimes(\vec b\cdot\hat{\vec\sigma})\rangle = -\vec a\cdot\vec b$. With coplanar settings $\vec a = \hat z$, $\vec a' = \hat x$, $\vec b = (\hat z + \hat x)/\sqrt2$, $\vec b' = (\hat z - \hat x)/\sqrt2$,
$$S = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} = -2\sqrt2, \qquad |S| = 2\sqrt2 > 2 .$$
:::

::: {.theorem name="Tsirelson bound"}
For any quantum state and any $\pm1$-valued observables, $|S| \leq 2\sqrt2$.
:::

*Proof sketch.* With $\hat C = \hat A\otimes(\hat B + \hat B') + \hat A'\otimes(\hat B - \hat B')$ and $\hat A^2 = \hat B^2 = \hat{\mathbb 1}$, one computes $\hat C^2 = 4\hat{\mathbb 1} - [\hat A, \hat A']\otimes[\hat B, \hat B']$, whose norm is at most $8$; hence $|\langle\hat C\rangle| \leq 2\sqrt2$. $\square$

Quantum correlations are therefore **nonlocal**: no local realistic model reproduces them. They are also limited — the no-signalling principle alone would allow $|S| = 4$ ("PR boxes").
