15  Life at the micro-scale: information, ratchets and molecular motors

Inside a cell, molecular machines transport cargo, copy DNA and contract muscles, all while being kicked around by thermal fluctuations with energies comparable to their own working energy. Can such machines extract work from noise, apparently violating the second law? This chapter revisits the paradoxes of Maxwell’s demon and Feynman’s ratchet, introduces Landauer’s principle (information is physical), and builds the stochastic models of molecular and Brownian motors. Their consistency with thermodynamics rests on the principle of local detailed balance.

15.1 Energy scales of the cell

At \(T = 300\) K, the thermal energy is \(k_BT = R T/N_A = 4.1\times10^{-21}\) J \(\simeq 4.1\) pN·nm \(\simeq 0.6\) kcal/mol \(\simeq 25\) meV. Molecular processes involve energies of a few to a few tens of \(k_BT\):

  • ATP hydrolysis, \(\text{ATP} \rightleftharpoons \text{ADP} + \text{P}_i\), releases \(\Delta G^0 \simeq -30\) kJ/mol \(\simeq 5\times10^{-20}\) J \(\simeq 12\,k_BT\) under standard conditions, and about \(20\)–\(25\,k_BT\) under cellular conditions, where ATP is far from equilibrium with its products.
  • A water molecule at \(300\) K moves at \(\sqrt{\langle v^2\rangle} = \sqrt{3RT/M} \simeq 650\) m/s and undergoes a collision every \(\sim 10^{-13}\) s. A protein in water experiences \(\sim 10^{13}\) collisions per second, so its motion is overdamped Brownian motion: inertia is irrelevant (low Reynolds number), and any directed motion must be produced against incessant random forces.

15.2 Maxwell’s demon and the cost of information

Maxwell (1867) imagined a demon controlling a trapdoor between two gas compartments at the same temperature. Letting fast molecules pass only from left to right and slow ones from right to left, it would create a temperature difference, \(T_R > T_L\), without doing work, decreasing the entropy of an isolated system. This contradicts the second law.

Szilard’s engine (1929) makes it quantitative with one molecule in a box at temperature \(T\). Insert a partition in the middle, measure on which side the molecule is (one bit of information), and let it push the partition isothermally to the far wall. The extracted work is \[W = \int_{V/2}^Vp\,dV = k_BT\ln2,\] seemingly from a single heat bath.

The resolution came from the physics of computation. Bennett (1982) showed that measurement can be done without dissipation, but the demon’s memory must eventually be reset to be reused, and:

Erasing one bit of information in an environment at temperature \(T\) dissipates at least \[Q \geq k_BT\ln2,\] i.e. it increases the entropy of the environment by at least \(\Delta S \geq k_B\ln2\).

Including the memory, the total entropy balance \(\Delta S_{\text{gas}} + \Delta S_{\text{memory}} + \Delta S_{\text{environment}} \geq 0\) is respected: the work extracted by the Szilard engine is paid for by erasing the measurement record. Information is physical. Landauer’s bound was verified experimentally with a colloidal particle in a double-well optical trap (Bérut et al., 2012): erasing the bit (forcing the particle into one well, whatever the initial well) dissipates on average \(k_BT\ln2\) in the slow limit. Conversely, an information engine can convert information into work at most \(k_BT\ln2\) per bit.

15.3 Feynman’s ratchet and pawl

Feynman (1962) analysed a microscopic ratchet: a paddle wheel immersed in a gas at temperature \(T_2\) is connected by an axle to a ratchet with a spring-loaded pawl at temperature \(T_1\). The pawl lets the ratchet rotate only one way, so it seems that random collisions with the paddles would rotate the axle and lift a weight.

They do not when \(T_1 = T_2\). The pawl itself is thermally agitated: with the same probability \(\propto e^{-\epsilon/k_BT}\) with which a collision supplies the energy \(\epsilon\) to advance one tooth, the pawl is thermally lifted and the ratchet slips back. Forward and backward rates balance, and no net motion occurs. If \(T_2 > T_1\), the device works as a heat engine, with efficiency bounded by Carnot’s, \(\eta \leq 1 - T_1/T_2\). (Feynman’s original claim that it reaches the Carnot efficiency is incorrect: heat conduction through the axle makes it irreversible; Parrondo and Español, 1996.) Rectifying equilibrium fluctuations is impossible; directed motion needs a source of free energy that drives the system out of equilibrium.

15.4 Brownian motors

15.4.1 Equilibrium in a periodic potential

Consider an overdamped particle in a periodic, asymmetric (“sawtooth”) potential \(V(x + L) = V(x)\). At equilibrium its density is the Boltzmann distribution \(P_{\text{eq}}(x) \propto e^{-\beta V(x)}\). On an infinite line the normalisation diverges, but the reduced density \[\hat P(x) = \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}P(x + nL), \qquad \hat P_{\text{eq}}(x) = \frac{e^{-\beta V(x)}}{\hat Z}, \qquad \hat Z = \int_0^Le^{-\beta V(x)}dx,\] is well defined on one period. Being an equilibrium state, it carries zero current, \(J = 0\): asymmetry of the potential alone does not produce motion. With an external force \(F\), the tilted potential \(V(x) - Fx\) produces a drift in the direction of the force, whatever the asymmetry.

15.4.2 Rectifying noise out of equilibrium

Directed motion appears when the potential is switched or tilted in time, which requires an energy input. In a flashing ratchet the sawtooth is periodically turned on and off. While off, the particle diffuses symmetrically. When the potential is switched on again, the particle is captured in the well toward which it has a shorter distance to the nearest maximum. Because of the asymmetry, more particles cross the short side than the long side, and a net current flows, even against a small load. A rocking ratchet (a periodic, zero-mean tilt) or temperature oscillations work the same way. Brownian motors are theoretical and artificial devices (optical ratchets, colloids, electrons in asymmetric structures) that turn unbiased fluctuations into directed motion with the help of a non-equilibrium driving.

15.5 Molecular motors

15.5.1 Biological motors

Cells contain three families of molecular motors, proteins that convert the chemical free energy of ATP into mechanical work:

  • kinesins walk along microtubules toward their plus end, carrying vesicles in axons, in \(8\) nm steps, one ATP per step, at about \(1\,\mu\)m/s;
  • dyneins walk toward the minus end of microtubules (retrograde transport, cilia and flagella);
  • myosins move on actin filaments (muscle contraction, transport).

Compared with Brownian motors, they are tightly coupled to chemistry: each step is associated with a conformational change triggered by binding and hydrolysis of ATP. Directionality comes from the asymmetric structure of the track and of the motor, and their motion is nearly deterministic at high ATP concentration.

15.5.2 A two-state model

The simplest model has the motor on a periodic track with sites \(x = na\) and two internal states, \(1\) and \(2\) (e.g. ATP-free and ATP-bound). The transitions are:

  • chemical: \(1 \to 2\) by ATP binding/hydrolysis, at rate \(K_+\); backward at rate \(K_-\), both at the same site;
  • mechanical: from state \(2\) at site \(n\) to state \(1\) at site \(n + 1\) at rate \(W_+\), and backward at rate \(W_-\).

For the site-summed probabilities \(P_i(t) = \sum_nP_i(n, t)\), \[\dot P_1 = (K_- + W_+)P_2 - (K_+ + W_-)P_1, \qquad P_1 + P_2 = 1,\] so at stationarity \[P_2^* = \frac{K_+ + W_-}{K_+ + K_- + W_+ + W_-}.\] The mean velocity is \(a\) times the net rate of forward mechanical steps: \[v = a\bigl(W_+P_2^* - W_-P_1^*\bigr) = a\,\frac{W_+K_+ - W_-K_-}{K_+ + K_- + W_+ + W_-}.\] The motor moves forward iff \(W_+K_+ > W_-K_-\): the product of the forward rates around a cycle must exceed that of the backward rates. In equilibrium the two products are equal (Kolmogorov’s criterion for detailed balance), and \(v = 0\).

15.5.3 Local detailed balance and thermodynamic consistency

How are the rates constrained by thermodynamics? For a transition \(x' \to x\) that exchanges heat with a bath, local detailed balance requires \[\frac{W_{x'\to x}}{W_{x\to x'}} = e^{Q_{\text{bath}}/k_BT} = e^{\Delta S_{\text{bath}}/k_B},\] where \(Q_{\text{bath}}\) is the heat released to the environment. By the first law, \(\Delta E = Q + W + \sum_i\mu_i\Delta N_i\), where \(W\) is mechanical work done on the system and the last term is the chemical work. Over a full cycle the motor returns to the same internal state, advances by \(a\) against a load \(F\) (work \(-Fa\)), and converts one ATP into ADP and \(\text{P}_i\), with \(\Delta\mu = \mu_{\text{ATP}} - \mu_{\text{ADP}} - \mu_{\text{P}_i}\). Hence \[\frac{W_+K_+}{W_-K_-} = e^{\beta(\Delta\mu - Fa)}.\]

  • With \(\Delta\mu = 0\) and \(F = 0\) (equilibrium), forward and backward cycles are equally likely, and \(v = 0\).
  • The motor moves forward iff \(\Delta\mu > Fa\). The stall force is \(F_s = \Delta\mu/a\); beyond it the motor walks backward, and at high enough force it can even synthesise ATP. For kinesin (\(a = 8\) nm, \(\Delta\mu \simeq 20\)–\(25\,k_BT\)) this gives \(F_s \simeq 10\)–\(13\) pN, while measured stall forces are \(6\)–\(7\) pN: kinesin works at an efficiency of about \(50\%\).
  • The entropy production per cycle is \(\beta(\Delta\mu - Fa) \geq 0\), and the thermodynamic efficiency \(\eta = Fa/\Delta\mu \leq 1\), with equality only at stall, where the speed vanishes.

These are instances of stochastic thermodynamics, which extends the first and second laws to single trajectories of small systems. One of its results is that the ratio of the probabilities of a trajectory and its time reverse equals \(e^{\Delta S_{\text{tot}}/k_B}\) (fluctuation theorems).

15.6 Exercises

(Szilard engine) Compute the work extracted by isothermal expansion of a one-molecule gas from \(V/2\) to \(V\), and the minimal heat dissipated by resetting the memory. Express both at \(300\) K in joules and in units of the ATP free energy.

Solution. \(W = \int_{V/2}^V\frac{k_BT}{V'}dV' = k_BT\ln2 = 2.9\times10^{-21}\) J at \(300\) K. Resetting costs \(Q \geq k_BT\ln2\), the same amount, so the cycle produces no net work, as the second law requires. ATP releases about \(5\times10^{-20}\) J (\(12\,k_BT\), standard conditions), so one ATP could pay for erasing about \(17\) bits at the Landauer limit. Real cellular information processing (e.g. sensing by receptors) dissipates many times more per bit.

(No current at equilibrium) For an overdamped particle in a periodic potential, the Fokker–Planck current is \(J = -D\bigl(\partial_xP + \beta V'(x)P\bigr)\). Show that the stationary periodic solution has \(J = 0\) when \(V(x + L) = V(x)\), and compute \(J\) for a tilted potential \(V(x) - Fx\) to first order in \(F\).

Solution. At stationarity \(J\) is constant. Write \(J = -De^{-\beta V}\partial_x(e^{\beta V}P)\), so \(\partial_x(e^{\beta V}P) = -\frac JDe^{\beta V}\). Integrating over a period with \(P\) and \(V\) periodic, the left side integrates to zero, while the right side integrates to \(-\frac JD\int_0^Le^{\beta V}dx \neq 0\). Hence \(J = 0\) and \(P \propto e^{-\beta V}\). With a tilt, \(e^{\beta(V - Fx)}P\) increases by the factor \(e^{-\beta FL}\) over a period, and to first order in \(F\) the particle drifts with velocity \(v = \beta D_{\text{eff}}F\), where \(D_{\text{eff}} = \frac{DL^2}{\int_0^Le^{\beta V}dx\int_0^Le^{-\beta V}dx} \leq D\) (the Lifson–Jackson formula). The drift is along the force, with a mobility reduced by the potential barriers, whatever the asymmetry of \(V\).

(Two-state motor) For \(K_+ = 100\), \(K_- = 1\), \(W_+ = 200\), \(W_- = 2\) (in s\(^{-1}\)) and \(a = 8\) nm, compute \(P_2^*\), the velocity, and the free energy per cycle \(k_BT\ln\frac{W_+K_+}{W_-K_-}\). What load stalls the motor?

Solution. Sum of rates \(= 303\) s\(^{-1}\). \(P_2^* = (100 + 2)/303 = 0.337\). \(v = 8\,\text{nm}\times\frac{200\times100 - 2\times1}{303} = 8\times66.0 = 528\) nm/s. Cycle affinity \(\ln(20000/2) = \ln10^4 = 9.2\), so \(\Delta\mu - Fa = 9.2\,k_BT\) at zero load. The stall force is \(F_s = 9.2\,k_BT/a = 9.2\times4.1/8 = 4.7\) pN. Under a load \(F\) the backward rates increase (or forward rates decrease) so that the ratio becomes \(e^{9.2 - Fa/k_BT}\), and \(v \to 0\) at \(F_s\).

(Efficiency of kinesin) Kinesin steps \(8\) nm per ATP and stalls at \(6\) pN under conditions where \(\Delta\mu = 25\,k_BT\). Compute the maximum mechanical work per step, the thermodynamic efficiency at stall, and the entropy produced per step at half the stall force.

Solution. Work at stall: \(6\,\text{pN}\times8\,\text{nm} = 48\,\text{pN·nm} = 11.7\,k_BT\). Efficiency \(\eta = 11.7/25 = 47\%\). At \(F = 3\) pN, the work per step is \(24\) pN·nm \(= 5.9\,k_BT\), and the entropy produced per step is \(\Delta S/k_B \geq 25 - 5.9 = 19\,k_BT\): most of the free energy is dissipated as heat when the motor moves at a finite speed.

Cycle affinity $\ln(20000/2) = \ln10^4 = 9.2$, so $\Delta\mu - Fa = 9.2\,k_BT$ at zero load. The stall force is $F_s = 9.2\,k_BT/a = 9.2\times4.1/8 = 4.7$ pN. Under a load $F$ the backward rates increase (or forward rates decrease) so that the ratio becomes $e^{9.2 - Fa/k_BT}$, and $v \to 0$ at $F_s$.
:::

::: {.exercise}
**(Efficiency of kinesin)** Kinesin steps $8$ nm per ATP and stalls at $6$ pN under conditions where $\Delta\mu = 25\,k_BT$. Compute the maximum mechanical work per step, the thermodynamic efficiency at stall, and the entropy produced per step at half the stall force.
:::

::: {.solution}
Work at stall: $6\,\text{pN}\times8\,\text{nm} = 48\,\text{pN·nm} = 11.7\,k_BT$. Efficiency $\eta = 11.7/25 = 47\%$. At $F = 3$ pN, the work per step is $24$ pN·nm $= 5.9\,k_BT$, and the entropy produced per step is $\Delta S/k_B \geq 25 - 5.9 = 19\,k_BT$: most of the free energy is dissipated as heat when the motor moves at a finite speed.
:::
